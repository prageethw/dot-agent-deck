## A worker pane's outstanding delegation/silence watch no longer sits armed for its full timeout after the worker's process has already exited

When a delegated worker's process ended on its own — finished its task and its session simply exited — without ever calling `work-done` and without an explicit `StopAgent` close, its armed idle-worker delegation (PRD #126) and delegate-silence watch (PRD #249) had no way to learn the process was gone. Both mechanisms are cancelled only by `work-done` or by an explicit close; neither one asked the daemon's own reader thread, which already knows the moment a pane's PTY reaches EOF that its process has died. The records sat armed until their full timeout window elapsed — 120 minutes and 30 seconds by default — indistinguishable in the meantime from a worker that was merely slow.

`pump_reader`'s EOF branch (`src/agent_pty.rs`) now retires any armed `OutstandingDelegation`/`SilenceWatchRecord` for that pane the moment PTY EOF is observed, via a new `AgentPtyRegistry::sweep_delegations_on_exit` that reuses the same drain logic an explicit `StopAgent` close already applies — without that close path's `closing_panes`/`close_waiters` side effects, which have no natural "finish" moment for a process that died on its own. Retirement is idempotent, so a late, legitimate `work-done` racing the same EOF is safe either way.

This is issue #465's M1 only: it stops both records from lingering past a worker's actual exit, but does not yet add a new notice telling the orchestrator the worker exited without reporting — that lands in a follow-up milestone. In the window between this change and that one, a worker that exits silently produces no notice at all (previously it would eventually still surface the existing timeout-based prompt, just very late); the follow-up milestone closes that gap with a dedicated, fixed-text notice.
