Hardened the process scan behind the shell-activity signal, which promotes a pane to **Working** while a foreground shell command runs in it. The scan samples the machine's process table with `ps` twice a second, and that call previously had no time limit and ran directly on one of the daemon's async worker threads. On a machine where `ps` was slow or wedged — a stalled network filesystem, extreme load — the worker parked with it, so status updates, client requests and shutdown all waited behind a process listing.

The sample is now taken off the worker threads and given a two-second budget; a sample that overruns is abandoned and its `ps` killed. A sample that times out or fails reports *no opinion* rather than "nothing is busy", so a struggling machine leaves pane statuses exactly as they were instead of silently flipping busy panes back to `Idle`.
